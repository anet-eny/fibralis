The challenge

Closing the gap between biology and scalable production

Native collagen derives its biological activity from a precise triple-helical fold — a conformation that depends critically on post-translational hydroxylation of proline residues. This modification does not occur in E. coli, and without it the triple helix unfolds at physiological temperature, losing both structural integrity and the receptor-binding geometry that defines its bioactivity. For decades, this single biochemical constraint forced the field to choose between functional collagen derived from animal tissue — with all its variability and safety concerns — and recombinant systems that could not reliably replicate the fold.

Fibralis resolves this through sequence-level engineering. Drawing on the systematic mapping of bioactive motifs across collagen type I and III sequences (He et al., 2025; Xu & Kirchner, 2021), and integrating AI-assisted design into the full DBTL cycle, we construct collagen-mimetic molecules de novo: sequences that are not found in nature in this exact form, but are built from validated, literature-confirmed active sites — integrin-binding GFOGER-family motifs, DDR-activating sequences, signalling peptide cores with documented fibroblast activity. Hydroxyproline-dependent positions are replaced by strategically placed (Gly-Pro-Pro)ₙ repeats and inter-chain salt bridges; terminal folding is driven by nucleation domains that enforce correct chain register without enzymatic co-factors. The result is a molecule that folds correctly in E. coli, can be produced at fermentation scale, and retains the receptor-level bioactivity of its native counterparts.

Every molecule we advance is subjected to a rigorous characterisation cascade before any biological claim is made. Structural integrity is confirmed by CD spectroscopy — the gold standard for triple-helix validation — combined with DSF thermostability profiling and FTIR hydrogen-bond analysis. Receptor binding affinity is quantified by SPR against integrin I-domains and relevant collagen receptors. Biological activity is assessed in human adult dermal fibroblasts using dose-response designs across 72-hour incubation windows, with collagen secretion measured by Sircol assay and proCollagen I ELISA, mRNA expression of COL1A1, COL1A2 and fibronectin confirmed by RT-qPCR with protein-level verification, and downstream signalling through pSmad2/3 and pERK1/2 resolved by Western blot. We follow the best-practice framework outlined in Van Walraven et al. (2025), including standardised molar concentrations, age-matched cell models, and positive controls in every experiment.

"Active sites with proven receptor engagement, delivered in molecules that E. coli can make at scale — that is what rigorous sequence engineering looks like."

Our approach

How we engineer collagen that works

We combine sequence engineering, structural domain design, and optimised E. coli expression to produce bioactive collagen molecules that are reproducible, animal-free, and functionally equivalent to their human counterparts.

Image placeholder
01

Hydroxylation-independent triple-helix stability

Rational substitution of hydroxyproline-dependent positions with (Gly-Pro-Pro)ₙ repeat motifs enables stable triple-helix formation in E. coli at physiological temperature — without any enzymatic modification.

02

Nucleation domain engineering

Fusion of trimerisation-inducing domains at the termini of the collagen sequence drives spontaneous and efficient triple-helix folding, increasing the yield of correctly assembled trimeric product.

03

Sequence-defined bioactivity

Every molecule is engineered at the sequence level to display defined integrin-binding, cell-signalling, or matrix-scaffolding activity — enabling targeted biological function without batch-to-batch variability.

Lead optimisation

GLOGER/GAOGER integrin mimic

α1β1-selective · COL1A1-derived

De novo collagen-mimetic peptides built on the GLOGER and GAOGER scaffolds — high-affinity sites within the α1 chain of collagen I. Unlike GFOGER, which preferentially engages α2β1, these sequences show selectivity for the α1β1 integrin subtype, expressed predominantly on fibroblasts in inflammatory and fibrotic states. A selective α1β1 agonist opens a mechanistic route to anti-scarring formulations not accessible through existing integrin-targeting peptides.

NTx-derived pro-angiogenic peptide

αvβ3-targeting · N-telopeptide origin

A bioactive peptide derived from the N-telopeptide region of collagen I, engineered to engage αvβ3 integrin — expressed on endothelial cells and regulating angiogenesis and neovascularisation. While NTx fragments are established bone resorption biomarkers, their activity at αvβ3 has not been exploited therapeutically. A de novo αvβ3-targeting collagen peptide addresses pro-angiogenic signalling in wound care and skin nutrition, complementing the existing portfolio without overlap.

GPOGES — first COL1A2-derived peptide

α1β1-selective · COL1A2 α2 chain

The vast majority of commercial collagen peptides are derived exclusively from the COL1A1 α1 chain. GPOGES is the functional equivalent of GFOGER in the COL1A2 α2 chain — with a distinct integrin binding profile favouring α1β1 over α2β1. As a selective modulator of fibroblast activation in inflammatory contexts, GPOGES represents an underexplored source of patentable peptide chemistry and a differentiated claim for anti-inflammatory skin formulations.

GPOGPSGPR — collagen III DDR1 mimic

DDR1-activating · COL3A1-derived

Collagen type III is the predominant collagen of foetal and juvenile skin — the molecular basis of its elasticity and resilience. As the COL1:COL3 ratio shifts with age, skin loses pliability. GPOGPSGPR is a sequence motif from the COL3A1 helix activating DDR1, a receptor tyrosine kinase expressed on epithelial cells regulating the basement membrane. A peptide stimulating COL3A1-associated signalling carries a substantiated "restore juvenescent collagen profile" claim — currently absent from the market.
